# IP protection and first-use terms — requirements draft

Status: queued for legal/ownership review, not an activated agreement. The owner
requested protections against copying, reverse engineering, redistribution and
imitation alongside the initial health/legal notice. No claim that MENEI is novel,
patentable, registered or immune to imitation has been verified.

## Protection goals and limits

- Identify the legal owner and protectable original code, branding, artwork and
  documentation. Verify ownership, assignments and any AI-generated/third-party
  material before claiming exclusive rights.
- Draft a limited license to use the app, with restrictions on unauthorized
  copying, resale, redistribution, scraping of protected app content, removal of
  notices, and reverse engineering to the extent applicable law allows. Preserve
  mandatory statutory exceptions and third-party/open-source license rights.
- Preserve users' rights to their own records and existing backup/export/import
  workflows. Restrictions on app IP must not prohibit a user exporting their
  workout, nutrition or account data.
- Protect MENEI name/logo and confusingly similar branding where legally
  available. Trademark clearance/registration and any copyright registration
  require separate professional review; none has been performed or filed here.
- Evaluate whether any qualifying technical invention merits patent advice.
  Public disclosure can affect patent options and deadlines; terms cannot create
  a patent or exclusive rights to an app idea or general fitness functionality.
- Terms bind only where validly agreed and enforceable. They cannot guarantee
  prevention of copying or lawful independent development of similar features.

## Existing exposure and technical review

The existing GitHub app repository is public, as recorded in the preservation
handoff. No top-level LICENSE/COPYING file was found in this checkout; that alone
does not establish ownership, absence of other licenses, or revocation of rights
already granted. Review repository history, prior releases, GitHub terms,
third-party code/fonts/assets and prior disclosures before selecting a license.
The bundled Cinzel font's separate license must remain intact.

Browser code and downloadable assets can be inspected. Minification, obfuscation
or disabling context menus cannot provide an absolute anti-copying guarantee.
Keep API keys and confidential backend logic server-side; review future
architecture changes without breaking approved offline behavior or data recovery.
No repository sharing/access, source license, media notices, backend or runtime
secret was changed as part of this requirements draft.

## First-use page integration to prepare after review

1. Confirm rights-holder/legal entity, governing jurisdiction, contact and the
   treatment of existing users and previously released code.
2. Have counsel review health/injury/allergy language, IP license/restrictions,
   privacy, consumer rights and enforceability together. Avoid guarantees of
   liability immunity or prevention of imitation.
3. Present readable Terms and Privacy links with separate explicit acceptance
   where required; version accepted terms and retain only necessary acceptance
   evidence. The current health-notice acknowledgment is not a substitute for a
   reviewed IP agreement. Do not silently treat earlier acknowledgment as consent
   to new restrictions.
4. Keep the terms available in Settings, with accessible layout and a clear update
   policy. Prepare any beta confidentiality agreement separately if needed; do
   not claim a publicly disclosed idea is confidential retroactively.
5. Preserve existing app/data identities, backups, original approvals and user
   records. Test any acceptance-state changes separately before public release.

This draft creates no registration, filing, legal opinion, new third-party
contract, source-access change or promise of exclusivity.
